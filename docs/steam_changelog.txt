[h1]Balance — 6.5 mm now sits properly between 5.56 and 7.62[/h1]

The 6.5x39 caseless family was never a real middle step. It hit for the same as 5.56 while carrying a 7.62-weight bullet, and its ballistic coefficients were flatter than 7.62 outright — so 6.5 reached further than the round it's meant to sit under. Penetration wandered in both directions too: Mk328 pierced like 5.56, while XM892 and XM893 out-pierced every 7.62 load in the pack.

Every West 6.5 load is now pinned to the midpoint of its 5.56 and 7.62 counterpart — damage, penetration, muzzle velocity, drag, bullet weight and BC. In practice 6.5 now lands around 2.9-3.3 kJ at the muzzle, against 1.8-1.9 kJ for 5.56 and 3.9-4.2 kJ for 7.62: clearly better than the little round, clearly short of the big one.

[list]
[*][b]Mk330 EPR[/b] — the general-purpose load, dead centre of the band
[*][b]Mk328 HV[/b] / [b]XM892 CTEP[/b] — the carbine loads
[*][b]Mk329 LR[/b] / [b]XM893 CTEP[/b] — the 610 mm DMR and MG loads, top of the band
[*][b]Mk331 AP[/b] — still the hardest-hitting on armour and the slowest of the six
[/list]

The four extra loads (Mk328, XM892, Mk329, XM893) also picked up the full ACE ballistics data the rest of the pack already had — velocity spread, atmosphere model and proper barrel-length curves instead of a single data point — so ACE Advanced Ballistics and ATragMX now read them the same way as everything else.

CSAT's Type 41 / 42 / 43 keep their usual ~10% gap under the NATO equivalents, so the faction balance is unchanged. The 6.2 mm caseless line is untouched, and no magazine or ammo classes were added, renamed or removed — existing loadouts and missions keep working.

[h1]Balance — 5.56 / 7.62 PAB fuze radii halved[/h1]

Realism pass: the Mk361 (5.56) and Mk362 (7.62) proximity rounds now need a genuinely close pass — trigger/lethal radius drops to 0.875/0.625 m and 1/0.75 m. Damage and reach unchanged; bigger calibers untouched. If you want forgiving fuzes, that's what the 40mm Mk364 — or the new buckshot below — is for.

[h1]New — Rifle anti-drone buckshot (Mk368 / Mk369 AD)[/h1]

Buckshot out of your rifle: two new anti-drone shot families that need no fuze, no script and no dialing — just point at the drone and fire. Each round splits into a tight tungsten pellet cone at the muzzle, holding a one-foot pattern radius out to its effective range.

[list]
[*][b]5.56 Mk368K AD[/b] — 8 pellets, effective to 100 m (2,200 fps)
[*][b]5.56 Mk368L AD[/b] — 5 heavier pellets, effective to 200 m
[*][b]7.62 Mk369K AD[/b] — 12 pellets, effective to 150 m (2,100 fps)
[*][b]7.62 Mk369L AD[/b] — 10 heavier pellets, effective to 250 m
[/list]

K is your close-in swarm answer; L trades pellet count for reach. They feed the standard STANAG 5.56 and 7.62 battle-rifle wells, and — with the JCA pack — all five JCA 5.56 mag bodies (EMAG, sand EMAG, PMAG, sand PMAG, Green PMAG) and both JCA 7.62 PMAGs. No tracer variants: the round splits at the muzzle. The JCA 5.56 and 7.62 range signs list the new loads (and the 7.62 board finally shows its Mk362 PAB row).

[h1]Balance — PAB anti-drone rounds toned down[/h1]

The rifle and MG proximity-airburst rounds (Mk361/362/363/366/367/373, PABS slug, DBJ-25, 7N42, 7N62) were doing too much work: fuze radius and damage are both halved.
